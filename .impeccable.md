## Design Context

### Users
KALLOS serves two distinct audiences inside one brand system.

- Primary audience: style-conscious D2C shoppers browsing a fashion-led commerce experience with apparel, accessories, and product storytelling that should feel closer to collectible objects than commodity basics.
- Shopper context: they are discovering, evaluating, and buying pieces online, often through editorial browsing rather than purely utilitarian search.
- Secondary audience: a small internal admin team, currently centered around a single operator, managing products, inventory, orders, promotions, reviews, and store settings.
- Job to be done: help customers feel they are acquiring a personal artifact with attitude and taste, while helping admins run the business with clarity and speed.

### Brand Personality
The brand personality is Gothic, Cool, Aesthetic.

- Voice and tone: poised, mysterious, self-assured, slightly poetic, and visually sharp.
- Emotional goal for customers: desire, intrigue, confidence, and a sense of ceremony.
- Emotional goal for admins: calm control, trust, and operational clarity.
- KALLOS should feel intentionally dark-edged and art-directed, not cute, bubbly, or mass-market.

### Aesthetic Direction
KALLOS should follow a hybrid direction: editorial luxury as the structural base, with darker avant-garde energy layered into the brand edge.

- Current codebase signals to preserve: serif plus sans typography, generous whitespace, refined motion, premium materials language, and clear functional separation between storefront and admin.
- Brand tension to resolve consistently: the storefront currently leans warm ivory and gold luxury, while the logo introduces a sharper crimson, black, and ritual-symbol language. Future work should integrate both rather than choosing only one.
- Visual references to draw from:
  - Macabre Gadgets: talismanic product storytelling, ritual-object framing, poetic copy, and ceremonial presentation.
  - Daemon Concept: otherworldly luxury, sharp silhouettes, biological-meets-futurist form language, and controlled dramatic tone.
- Anti-references:
  - Generic minimal DTC storefronts that feel interchangeable or startup-polished.
  - Costume-goth or Halloween-coded visuals.
  - Soft, overly romantic luxury that sands off the brand's edge.
  - Loud cyberpunk clutter, neon overload, or gimmicky dark UI tropes.
- Theme guidance: dark-first in emotional language and accent usage, with light surfaces allowed where commerce clarity and admin readability benefit from them.
- Color guidance: keep crimson as a precise high-intent accent, not a blanket wash. Favor black, charcoal, ivory, bone, muted metals, and controlled warm neutrals over candy colors or generic purple gradients.

### Design Principles
- Treat products like artifacts, not inventory. Product presentation should imply rarity, symbolism, craft, and personal attachment.
- Balance restraint with menace. Layouts should remain spacious and composed, but typography, imagery, and accents should carry a sharper gothic edge.
- Use contrast surgically. Let neutral fields carry readability while crimson, shadow, and sharp form language create tension at focal points.
- Separate romance from operations. The storefront can be atmospheric and emotive; the admin should stay brisk, disciplined, and branded without becoming theatrical.
- Keep motion ceremonial and controlled. Prefer smooth, confident reveals and depth over playful motion; always preserve reduced-motion fallbacks.
